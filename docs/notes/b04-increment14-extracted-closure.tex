\section{Fixed-normal graph theorem}

Let \(c\) be the cell center. Choose \(e\) in the direction of the midpoint
of the certified center-gradient rectangle and let \(t\perp e\).
The interval replay proves throughout the whole cell
\[
f_e\ge m_C>0,\qquad |f_t|\le B_C,
\qquad \|D^2f\|_{\rm op}\le H_C.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Cell graph and chord error]
The unique zero arc in \(C\) is a graph
\[
u=\psi(v)
\]
in coordinates \(x=c+ue+vt\). Put
\[
S_C=\frac{B_C}{m_C}.
\]
Then
\[
|\psi'|\le S_C
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
|\psi''|
\le
M_C:=
\frac{H_C(1+S_C^2)}{m_C}.
}
\]

Let \(p_0,p_1\) be the exact boundary roots and \(q_0,q_1\) the midpoints of
their certified brackets. If \(\epsilon_i\) is the bracket half-width and
\[
L_C
=
|t\cdot(q_1-q_0)|+\epsilon_0+\epsilon_1,
\]
then the segment \(\widehat L_C=[q_0,q_1]\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\dH(\Gamma_0\cap C,\widehat L_C)
\le
\frac{M_CL_C^2}{8}
+
\max\{\epsilon_0,\epsilon_1\}.
}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since \(f_e>0\), the implicit function theorem and strict monotonicity along
every \(e\)-line make the unique arc a global graph over its \(v\)-projection.
Thus \(\psi'=-f_t/f_e\).

Along the graph let \(w=t+\psi'e\). Twice differentiating \(f=0\) gives
\[
0=w^TD^2f\,w+f_e\psi'',
\]
hence
\[
|\psi''|
\le
\frac{H_C\|w\|^2}{m_C}
\le
\frac{H_C(1+S_C^2)}{m_C}.
\]
The sharp linear-interpolation remainder for a \(C^2\) scalar graph gives
\(M_CL_C^2/8\) relative to the exact endpoint chord. Replacing exact roots
by bracket midpoints moves a chord by at most the larger endpoint error.
\end{proof}
